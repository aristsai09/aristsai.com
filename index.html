Hey! I’m Aris Tsai, a student researcher from Irvine, California, studying at Stanford Online High School and Sage Hill School. I have also pursued university-level coursework through Stanford, UCLA, Harvard, and UC Irvine. My work spans computational chemistry, molecular toxicology, genomics, structural biology, drug delivery, and biomedical engineering.

I’m especially interested in using computation to uncover mechanism rather than stopping at prediction. My projects range from an AI-based antibody-drug-conjugate toxicity predictor with greater than 90% predictive accuracy to continuum-solvated DFT, protonation-microstate modeling, mechanism-informed molecular fingerprints, molecular dynamics, large-scale genomics, and transporter-oriented levodopa delivery for Parkinson’s disease.

My research has been presented at ACS Spring, ACM CHI, the WACBE World Congress on Bioengineering, and the American Neurological Association Annual Meeting. Outside the lab, I lead STEM-access work through Trinity Academics, play competitive golf, and continue violin after performing with symphony ensembles including Pacific Symphony.

WHY I RESEARCH

Parkinson’s disease became personal before it became academic.

When I was in sixth grade, my father was diagnosed with Parkinson’s disease. Learning how little orally administered levodopa ultimately reaches the brain pushed me from textbooks and online courses into drug-delivery research.

That motivation anchors my work on levodopa delivery, where my project increased the estimated bioeffective fraction from about 5% to 9% — an ~80% relative increase, nearly doubling the starting value.

PROJECTS & MOTIVATION

Selected Research

NEUROLOGY · DRUG DELIVERY

Levodopa Delivery for Parkinson’s Disease

Motivated by my family’s experience with Parkinson’s disease, I investigated strategies to reduce peripheral levodopa loss and improve the fraction reaching the brain. The work presented at ANA2025 increased the estimated bioeffective fraction from ~5% to ~9%.

ANA2025 ↗

COMPUTATIONAL TOXICOLOGY

From Adverse Drug Reaction to Mechanism-Aware Prediction

An adverse reaction to polymyxin when I was younger pushed me toward drug-safety research. I later developed AI and molecular-representation approaches for predicting toxicity, including work on antibody-drug conjugates and mechanism-informed molecular fingerprints.

Google Scholar ↗

SYSTEMS MODELING · PUBLIC HEALTH

Anemia Intervention Model

I built a MATLAB/R simulation integrating biological mechanisms with malnutrition, care access, health infrastructure, economics, and behavioral variables. The model identified scenarios where iron supplementation alone would be insufficient without changes in delivery systems, subsidies, or behavior.
