Aris Tsai

computational chemistry + biology researcher

drug delivery · molecular toxicology · structural biology · biomedical computation

Hey! I’m Aris Tsai, a student researcher based in Irvine, California. My work sits at the intersection of computational chemistry, molecular toxicology, drug delivery, structural biology, genomics, and biomedical engineering. I’m especially interested in models that do more than predict an outcome — they should explain the molecular mechanism behind it and point toward a useful intervention.

In 2026, my work took me from ACS Spring in Atlanta to ACM CHI in Barcelona, the Wolfram High School Summer Research Program, Penn's Management & Technology Summer Institute (M&TSI), the 12th WACBE World Congress on Bioengineering in Changzhou, and selection to present at ANA2026 in San Diego.

My 2026 Google Scholar record spans machine-learning toxicity prediction, continuum-solvated DFT, protonation microstates, mechanism-informed molecular fingerprints, and molecular-dynamics analysis of PDE6B. Outside research, I work on STEM access through Trinity Academics, accessibility-focused service, violin, and golf.

why I research

Parkinson’s disease became personal before it became academic.

When I was in sixth grade, my father was diagnosed with Parkinson’s disease. Learning how little orally administered levodopa ultimately reaches the brain pushed me from textbooks and online courses into drug-delivery research.

That motivation now anchors my work on levodopa delivery, where a 2026 project increased the estimated bioeffective fraction from about 5% to 9% — an ~80% relative increase, nearly doubling the starting value.

selected research

neurology · drug delivery

Levodopa delivery for Parkinson’s disease

Motivated by my family’s experience with Parkinson’s disease, I investigated strategies to reduce peripheral levodopa loss and improve the fraction reaching the brain. The 2026 work selected for ANA2026 reports increasing the estimated bioeffective fraction from ~5% to ~9%.

computational toxicology

From an adverse drug reaction to mechanism-aware prediction

An adverse reaction to polymyxin when I was younger pushed me toward drug-safety research. I later developed AI and molecular-representation approaches for predicting toxicity, including work on antibody-drug conjugates and mechanism-informed molecular fingerprints.

systems modeling · public health

Anemia Intervention Model

I built a MATLAB/R simulation integrating biological mechanisms with malnutrition, care access, health infrastructure, economics, and behavioral variables. The model identified scenarios where iron supplementation alone would be insufficient without changes in delivery systems, subsidies, or behavior.
